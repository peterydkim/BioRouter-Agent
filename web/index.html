Capability is not permission

What is real here, stated plainly. The structure is real: the separation of permission from capability, the basis tags, the refusal to price a blocked path, the named-approver requirement, the freshness clock. The content is not: every verdict is a hypothesis derived from public regulatory text, every fit score is an assumption, and every cost figure in the underlying repository is a placeholder. The project's code has automated tests and CI, which establishes that the harness behaves as documented — not that any verdict here is correct for your company. Treat this as a worked argument about how such a system should be built, not as an answer about your estate.

Permission comes from policy and is binary-ish. Capability fit is a 1–5 score on a task. A platform can be fully permitted and bad at the job. These are never merged into one number, because the two require opposite responses — one is a legal conversation, the other is an engineering one.

What is not ready

  • The matrix is unsigned. No security, privacy, QA or legal reviewer has walked it line by line.
  • Nine open items are logged in the changelog, including which Data Use Agreements carry non-transferability clauses reaching model prompting — the cell most likely to be wrong in any real estate.
  • No jurisdiction axis. Several de-identified clinical cells likely split by jurisdiction and currently do not.
  • Zero measured capability scores. Every fit number is inference from vendor documentation, and is labelled as such.
  • Seven of ten of this project's own doctrinal claims are UNVERIFIED and tagged in its claims register rather than asserted.
  • No cost figures appear in this app at all. Every rate in the repository is a placeholder, and pricing a path nobody may use is how a governance tool becomes a liability.
